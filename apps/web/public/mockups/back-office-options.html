The back office, held together

Three ways to present a catalogue that is going to hold hundreds of thousands of locations — written against the real numbers on production this morning, not a sketch. Everything below is one argument: Atlas, Shelves and the sweep are not three things. They are one catalogue, one property of it, and one job that fills it.

What production actually says today

Pulled from /api/admin/library, /api/admin/scout/ and /api/places/area/SL4 on 5 Sep 2026. These are the numbers the screens have to make legible.

The atlas is 24% harvested and its pictures are 76% unattributed

7,461 attractions found, 921 published, 26 of 107 regions done, 80 never run. 2,524 pictures, 1,928 of them need a credit line, 371 MB held. 13 published places have no card image.

The Atlas screen shows all five of these as tiles. What it cannot do is tell you which 1,928 in one tap, or which regions the hole is in.

The sweep can read one menu in four

10 areas swept, 867 seen, 245 kept, 204 researched, 39 menus read, 3,821 dishes, 128 places with no menu. SL1 Slough: 25 kept, 2 read, 23 failed.

The Menus tab lists all 126 outstanding — but with 63 distinct free-text reasons ("Followed “Christmas Menu” on theswinleyascot.co.uk."). Only three are coded (menu_had_no_items, menu_unreadable, menu_url_required). A list of 63 sentences is an anecdote. It cannot be counted, charted, or fixed in priority order.

Our restaurant score is mostly Google's rating wearing a coat

score() weights the crowd at 0.5, accolades 0.3, substance 0.2. No Windsor restaurant has a single accolade, so accolade = 0 for all 25. Every one of the top 12 is banded "top". The spread across the whole first page is 6.7 → 5.8.

So the ranking is: Google's band (saturated), then how many dishes we read. owned_score is stored beside it and is not shown anywhere in the back office — which is the one number that would prove the ranking survives the key dying.

Everything you asked about the Atlas is a missing link, not missing data

"4 pictures" is real — GET /api/admin/library/images?subjectType=attraction&subjectId=… answers with all four, licence, credit, photographer and Commons URL. Nothing on the row calls it.

Pin = this row survives the next harvest's pruning and sorts to the top of its county (library.js:326, :346). Hide = state='hidden', dropped from ranking and from the app. Neither says so on screen, and neither is a per-fact rejection.

Why this shape

  • One table means one answer to "have we found everything". Two tables means two half-answers you have to add up in your head.
  • The saved view is the work queue. "Menu unread · 128" is a tab, not a report you go and read somewhere else.
  • The six dots are the whole quality model in 54 pixels. They sort, they filter, they roll up into Coverage.
  • Ours beside Composite makes the licensed dependency visible on every row. Antalya 3.4 / 6.7 says out loud: two thirds of that number is Google's.

The cost

  • Restaurants and attractions want different columns. One table means either sparse columns or a column set per saved view.
  • Merging attractions and scout_places is a migration, not a screen change. Two days, not two hours.

A · Places. Rows are real: Windsor Castle score 0.770 shown ×10, Antalya 6.7 composite against a computed owned score of ~3.4. Type, source and county are facets because those are the three ways a hole shows up.

Why this shape

  • Your real question is not "show me a place", it is "where are the holes". This makes the hole the first-class object.
  • The matrix is how data-quality dashboards are built everywhere else: completeness × segment, drill to the failing rows.
  • Recovery is the answer to your reporting ask. Eight coded causes replace 63 sentences, and each has one fix that moves a number.
  • "Seasonal-anchor bug: 17 places · one fix" is what a crawler backlog looks like when it is countable. Today it is invisible.

The cost

  • Needs a fact_coverage view and a coded failure taxonomy — the crawler must stop throwing prose.
  • It is a reporting screen. You still need Option A's table to actually curate a row.

B · Coverage. Every percentage is computed from production: SL4 has 16 of 25 menus (64%), SL1 has 2 of 25 (8%), Berkshire's 20 published all have a picture but 22% carry a credit line. The eight causes are the 63 free-text reasons grouped by hand — which is the point.

Save the verdict, not the figures

What the bench would actually tell you today

  • Every one of Windsor's 25 bands "top". A four-word band over a range where every restaurant scores 4.5–4.8 carries almost no information. The bands need re-cutting against the real distribution, or the crowd term needs to be percentile-within-area.
  • Accolades are 30% of the score and zero for all 25. Outside Michelin territory that weight silently redistributes to nothing — the composite is effectively crowd 0.71 / substance 0.29.
  • Substance is a proxy for crawler luck. 50nine ranks 9th on 5 dishes because the crawler found a drinks list. That is a scraping bug expressed as a food opinion.
  • All three are only visible because the two orderings sit next to each other. That is the argument for building this.

The rule this has to obey

  • Technical Constraints §4 forbids storing the figure. A bench that fetches, compares, displays and discards is inside that line — but it is your call, and the screen says so in the footer rather than hiding it.

The bench. Ranks are from production; Google's column is illustrative of what a live run returns. The verdict row — ρ, delta count, which places — is the only thing persisted.

What I would build, and what I need from you

Recommendation: B for the shell, A for the table, C's inspector

They are not really alternatives. Coverage is the landing page because your question is "where are the holes". Places is one list with type as a facet, because "have we found everything" must have one answer. The inspector is the same four tabs everywhere.

The rail becomes: Coverage · Places · Jobs & spend · Teaching, then the business half. Atlas, Shelves and the sweep stop being destinations.

Shelves is a field. The sweep is a job.

A shelf weight is a property of a location, so it belongs on the location — with a "rules you have taught" list under Teaching beside the extraction lessons, which are the same kind of object (a sentence you can argue with).

The sweep, the atlas harvest, the menu reads and the picture passes are four instances of one thing: a job that costs money and writes places. One Jobs & spend page, one cost line.

Three decisions that are yours, not mine

1 · Do we merge attractions and scout_places? Migration 044 already made attractions multi-source (Wikidata, Google, OSM). Merging is the honest end of that road and it is a day's migration with a backfill. Not merging is fine too — but then Coverage has to union them, and every new source needs a home decided in advance.

2 · May the bench show me Google's figure on screen? We never store it. Fetching it live, showing it in a table, and keeping only the verdict is my reading of §4 — but it is a licensing judgement and I would rather you make it than assume it.

3 · The crawler must stop throwing prose. 63 free-text reasons for 126 failures is why there is no recovery report today. Coding the causes (eight of them cover everything on production) is the smallest change with the biggest reporting payoff, and it is the prerequisite for the whole of Option B.

Quick wins that need no decision at all

• Make "4 pictures" a link — the endpoint already answers. • Label Pin and Hide with what they do. • Put owned_score next to roam_score everywhere a score is shown. • Show the website address as a column and a link. • Add "opening hours" to what the sweep researches — it is 0.10 of substance and null for every Windsor restaurant.
