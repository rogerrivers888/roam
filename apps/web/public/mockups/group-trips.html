Group trips — the organiser's six weeks, the participant's two minutes

The direction picked in the options page, drawn end to end: a group hangs off a trip, the organiser's screen is the work rather than the roster, and a participant joins from a link by giving a name at the first thing they tap. Same trip throughout — Cardiff · rugby tour, Fri 13 – Sun 15 November 2026, twenty-four expected, one hotel everybody books themselves, one stadium tour, and £45 each for the coach and match tickets Roger has already paid for. Light palette for the organiser, dark for the participant, because that is where each of them actually is.

No money moves on this page. Every screen here works with no payment provider, no partner agreement and no key: bookings are the participant's word until a partner can confirm them, and the fee is a line the organiser ticks. The two places where a Stripe account would change a screen are marked later.

What the organiser's half needs

  • One group per trip. A second attempt opens the first one.
  • A date to chase against. The doc leaves deadlines to v2; without one, “outstanding” has no urgency and the reminder has nothing to say. One date for the whole list is enough.
  • Reminder history per person, with what it was about — that is what stops him asking the same man twice and missing another entirely.
  • A private note. Half of what an organiser knows is “paying cash on the coach”.
  • Removing someone tells him what they paid and whether it comes back, and makes him confirm.

Not here, deliberately

  • No group chat. That is v3, and WhatsApp is not the thing Roam is replacing — the spreadsheet is.
  • No per-person amounts. One flat figure, in his words.

  • Her own checklist and her own bookings.

What changes at 1000px, and what does not

  • Does not: the blocks, their order, their buttons, the words in them, the selection, and every state the phone had.
  • Does: the roster becomes a table with a column per item, because the width is there and an organiser at a desk is reconciling, not chasing.
  • Sheets are the same component. The reminder sheet reads the frame's origin and size, so on the deployed site in Mobile it covers the phone and not the browser.
  • Row density matches Places and Trips: one line, a small avatar, a badge for anything that is not plain.

What is added to the offline policy

  • The participant's own checklist — their three items, their references, the dates.
  • The trip as they see it — where, when, the day plan, the addresses from the owned record.
  • And nothing else. Not the roster, not another person's state, not the fee's payment history for anyone but them.

What a group costs to run

  • Not eighteen times a trip. The trip's places are retrieved once and served to every participant from that pool; a participant's own browsing is the only thing that adds a call.
  • Every call a participant makes is logged against the group's trip and their session, so “what did this group cost” is a number the organiser's trip can already answer.
  • Reminders are the one outbound cost, and they are capped at one per person per item per two days.

What is yours to set up, not mine

  • The Stripe Connect platform account, and the connected-account model for organiser settlement.
  • Any key, in Doppler — never in the repo, never as a Railway variable.
  • Any spend or payout cap, provider-side.
  • The published refund terms and the participant privacy notice, both of which have to exist before a single fee is collected.

5 · What I would build, in what order

Six steps, each of which leaves the app working and useful. Nothing before step six needs a payment provider, a partner agreement or a key, which is the whole argument for this order.

#StepWhat it addsWhat it needs from you
1A group on a tripCreate, name, expected count, one group per trip, the trip reads as a group trip in the list. The dashed row at the bottom of a trip.Nothing
2The checklistMark the base and any stop as wanted; add one amount with a description and a refund rule; one date the whole list is chased against. Removing an item that people have done is blocked and says how many.Whether one date for the list is enough, or each item wants its own
3The link, and joiningShare, copy, code, add-by-name. Join with a name and one contact, bring heads, resume on a second open, regenerate, close, and match to a name the organiser added.Name + mobile, or name + email? One channel each
4The two viewsThe organiser's outstanding-first screen with the roster under it, phone and wide; the participant's three items. Bookings are self-declared, with a tracked hand-off where a partner exists.Nothing
5ChasingSelect, preview the words, send, record it against each person with a date and what it was about. The two-day hold. Reminder history on a person.The channel, and confirmation that Roam sends on the organiser's behalf at all
6The moneyVerification, the pay sheet, settlement to the organiser, refunds on withdrawal, and the “collection has stopped” state. No screen above changes — a tick becomes a button.Stripe Connect, verification, refund terms, privacy notice, and the platform fee

Two things worth adding that the doc does not ask for

  • The wrong-weekend catch. The trip knows the dates; a room booked for one night of two is caught in September. Free, because both facts are already there.
  • The kitchen's list. Three allergies among twenty-six covers, taken off the restaurant's own menu and sent with the booking. Also free, and it is the thing an organiser tells other people about.

Three things I would not build yet

  • Splitting one booking between four people. It needs Roam to be taking the money — v2, after step six.
  • Voting on the itinerary. The interesting half of planning as a group, and a different screen from a checklist. V3.
  • Group messaging. The chat already exists; the spreadsheet is what Roam is replacing.
