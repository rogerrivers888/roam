The back office, v2 — B on top, A underneath, and a place you can point at

Three changes to the design you picked: every number in Coverage is a doorway into the list, a county, a town or a postcode is a first-class object with its own page, and a correction is made where the mistake is, not on a screen somewhere else. Drawn against Windsor, because Windsor is where the atlas is most obviously wrong.

One thing to settle before this is built: geography is two trees, not one

A postcode district is not inside a county. SL4 spans five administrative districts — Windsor and Maidenhead, Slough, Bracknell Forest, Buckinghamshire and Runnymede — and reaches into Surrey. (That is the ONS's own answer from api.postcodes.io/outcodes/SL4, which sources/postcodeAreas.js already calls.)

So the picker offers two overlapping ladders, joined by a place's coordinates rather than nested inside each other: the administrative one (nation → county → town) and the postal one (area → district). Both land on the same kind of page. Pretending SL4 sits under Berkshire would put Ankerwycke Priory in the wrong county on screen, and it is already in the data twice over.

What makes it first-class

  • It has an address — /admin/places/windsor — so it obeys the working agreement that every page can be sent to somebody.
  • It has its own numbers: how much is here, how complete it is, what it cost, when it was last touched.
  • It holds both kinds of place at once. Windsor Castle and Antalya are both in Windsor; nothing about the harvest that found them should decide which page they appear on.
  • "What Windsor is made of" is the finding, not decoration. Zero family, zero active, zero animals out of 43, with Legoland mis-filed — that is the whole categorisation problem stated in one panel.

What it needs

  • A localities table and a locality on every place. Both fill from postcodes.io, which is keyless, free, ONS's own data, and already called by sources/postcodeAreas.js.
  • One reverse lookup per place — ?lon=&lat= returns the outcode and the admin district. 7,700 places is a few minutes of a background job, no key and no bill.

Windsor. Every figure is real: 43 attractions within 7km, 23 of them heritage, 100 pictures held, 25 restaurants, 16 menus read. "Kiosk To South East Of Cottage In Frogmore" and "M4 Thames Bridge" are genuinely in the atlas today.

A category change is free and reversible: attractions.kinds keeps the raw Wikidata types, so any reclassification can be replayed or undone from what is already stored.

Why this and not a dropdown

  • A dropdown fixes one row. The sentence fixes the type, and the type is where the mistake actually is — Legoland is filed landmark because amusement park is filed landmark.
  • It is the gesture that already exists. domain/teaching.js takes a sentence and returns a scoped proposal; this points it at place_kinds.category as well as shelf_rules.
  • "41 places, 19 counties" before you press Save is the thing a dropdown can never tell you.
  • It proposes and never writes — the rule that stopped silent guessing on the Shelves screen holds here too.

One honest caveat

  • Category and shelf are two different axes and must stay so. The panel shows both because a correction usually means both are wrong, but they are saved as two rules against two tables, and the screen says which is which.

What I need from you before building

The two ladders

Confirm that a place page should accept county, town and postcode district equally, with the postal and administrative ladders shown as two views of the same map rather than one nested tree. Everything else follows from that.

What a town is

postcodes.io gives an admin district ("Windsor and Maidenhead"), which is a council, not a town. For Windsor they differ. I would take the town from OSM's own place name via the existing Nominatim reverse lookup and keep the district beside it, so both are true and the page is titled the way a person would say it. Say if you would rather see the council name.

Scope of the first build

Coverage → Places click-through and the teach-in-place panel are both self-contained. The place tree is the larger piece because it needs the localities table and a backfill pass over 7,700 rows. I would do the two small ones first so you can use them this week, then the tree.
