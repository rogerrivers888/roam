Places tab — what it is, and three ways to lay it out

Your question was really three: what is a place, how should the Places tab be laid out (rows, not twenty pills), and where Places ends and Trips begins. The words come first, then three layouts that answer the third question differently, then the screen inside a destination (shared by all three) and a web layout. Rows with a chevron open and close.

Words first

Today the app says "place" for both London and Circolo Popolare, and "city" for a row you can type into. That is where the confusion starts. Four words, each with one meaning and one way of coming into being.

WordWhat it isHow it gets created
DestinationSomewhere you would say you are going: a city (London, Bath, Rome) or a named region (Lake District, Cotswolds). One London — everything inside Greater London files under it.By itself, the moment a trip is planned there or a visit is logged there. By hand only when you add one on purpose ("we're going to Lisbon"). Never from a bare search.
AreaA part of a destination: Soho, Greenwich, Richmond, the Bath city centre. Never its own row in the list — a label on a place, and a filter inside the destination.Read from the map data when the place is saved (the borough or district). Nothing for you to create.
PlaceA venue: a restaurant, a museum, a park. Lives inside one destination, carries the family's verdicts, Been / To try / Special.Any visit, save, special or shortlist entry writes it in. Or "Add a place" inside a destination for one you already know.
VisitOne dated occasion at a place, with who went and what each person thought."We've been here" on a place, or a trip stop marked as visited. Not a screen of its own in options B and C — the Been filter and the "last went" date do that job.
Seen in your live atlas today. Two rows called "United Kingdom" and "Italy" sit under those countries as if they were cities. They came from the Add box: typing a country name creates a "city" named after the country. That is the "do I create it?" confusion made concrete, and it goes away under every option below (a country typed into Add asks which city, or is refused). Greater London vs Outer London: Richmond and Greenwich are inside Greater London, so they are areas of London; Windsor and Ascot are outside, so they are their own destinations.

What changes

  • Country cards with city pills become one list: a country row that opens to its destinations. Counts read left to right: places, been, to try, special, trips.
  • Add moves up beside the title and opens the same city search as today. The sentence is one line.
  • Tapping a destination opens the dense list you already have (see "Inside a destination" below).

What stays

  • Find places and Visits as tabs, unchanged.
  • Trips keeps Up next / Later / Past.

Where it falls short

  • Every overlap in the table above survives. You still search from three doors and see "been" from three.

Option B — Separate more: Places remembers, a trip searches

Places is only the atlas: what you have been to, liked, and want to try. Nothing is searched here. Finding somewhere new always happens inside a trip (Plan makes one in a breath, even for tonight), so what you find lands on a shortlist and, once visited, back here. Visits fold into the atlas: a Been filter and a last-went date do the job, with the latest three at the top.

Country names are section labels, not rows to open; with one country they vanish.

What changes

  • No tabs on Places. The list is the destinations; the filter under it is Been / To try / Special across the whole atlas (so "everywhere we've been" is one tap, which is what Visits was for).
  • "Find places" leaves Places. Search is a trip's job: Plan › a trip › Find, near the base, saving to that trip's shortlist.
  • "Visits" leaves Places. Each destination row carries the last-went date; the three most recent visits sit at the top; inside a destination, rows carry Been N× and the date, as now.
  • Add asks for a city or region and refuses a country.

What stays

  • Trips keeps Up next / Later / Past. Past trips are the dated record; Places is the by-destination record.

Cost

  • A spontaneous "what's near here right now" needs a trip first. Plan already does this in one line ("dinner tonight near home"), so the extra step is small — but it is a step.

Option C — Join up: one row per destination holds its trips and its places

Your question "is Places just expired trips?" answered with yes, and more: a destination row shows what is coming up there, what happened there, and what you know there. Past trips move out of Trips into Places (Trips shows only what is ahead). Search still lives inside a trip, as in B.

What changes

  • The row is the destination, not the country; the country is a small suffix. Sort: soonest trip first, then most recently visited. Countries reappear as section labels only when the list passes about ten destinations.
  • Opening a row shows Trips here (upcoming then past), Our places with counts and "Open the list", and Plan a trip here.
  • Trips loses its Past fold and shows only Up next / Later. One line at the bottom: "Past trips live in Places".
  • Find and Visits leave Places, as in B.

Why this one

  • It answers your question directly: Trips is what's ahead, Places is where you've been and what you know. No job has two doors.
  • Going back somewhere starts from one row: what we liked, what's left, plan again.
Recommended: C, with B's rule that search lives inside a trip.

Area, not another destination

  • The area is the district the map data gives the place (today it is stripped when the city is folded to London; keeping it is one column, household_places.area).
  • The Area chip lists the areas with counts: Fitzrovia 1 · Trafalgar Square 1 · Westminster 1. It only appears when the destination has more than one area.
  • Same for Bath (city centre, Widcombe) and a region like the Lake District (Keswick, Ambleside, Windermere) — where the "cities" are areas of the destination you actually said you were going to.

Add a place here

  • Name search only (a place you already know), records it as To try or Been. It is not a discovery tool; that is a trip's Find.

Trips here

  • Under A and B this block still appears, but past trips also stay in the Trips tab. Under C this is their only home.

Web — Option C on a wide window

Three columns: destinations, the open destination, the map. The destination column is the same rows as the phone; the middle is the phone's inside-a-destination screen; the map stays on the right so the drawer (opening from the right) covers the map, not the list — the opposite of today's Places, which is why the map should move.
