Find — three ways to make it breathe

The Find tab as it should look: colour that says which kind of thing you are looking at, one control bar instead of rows of pills, the search behind a magnifier that opens when tapped, a budget you drag rather than read, a kind picker that opens big, and a "For you" strand that learns from what the family rates. No sentences of explanation anywhere. Sources are a desktop-only control in the top bar. Sibling pages: Shortlist → Trip · Trips tab.

Option 1 · Colour tiles and one bar

Three colour tiles pick the kind of thing. One bar underneath holds everything else: sort, kind, budget, distance, each in its own colour. Cards carry a photo, one line of facts and, when there is one, the reason it is for you.

Budget, opened. Easy, Medium, Splash out set the range; drag the ends to be exact. The same slider as the Plan screen. Free places always fit.

What goes

  • The second search box, the Refresh button and the fetched-at line. Refresh lives under the sliders icon.
  • Every explanatory sentence.
  • Rows of grey pills. The kind picker and the budget open as sheets.
  • Sources on the phone. On desktop they sit in the top bar.

For you

  • The default order. It ranks by the household's likes, favourites, allergens and dislikes, then by who is coming and their ages, then by past visits and takes. Each card shows the one reason that mattered.
  • It learns: every "We went" and every take moves the ranking. The old planner had this; it comes back here.

Option 2 · Strands, like a streaming home

No sort control at all. Find is a page of strands: For you, Top rated near here, Free, Been before, each a swipeable row of tiles. Filters are one sheet behind the sliders icon. Tapping a strand's title opens it as a full list.

Refresh from the sources

The one filter sheet: budget, kind, food, distance. Refresh is the last line here, not a button on the page.

Why this shape

  • Sorting is what the strands are. Nobody has to pick "Most reviewed" from a segmented control.
  • For you is always first and always visible, so the taste model earns its keep every time.
  • Fewer controls on the page: category pills, a magnifier, a sliders icon.

Trade-off

  • Horizontal rows show three at a time; a long scan means opening a strand.
  • What's on needs an event source switched on for its strand to have anything in it.

Option 3 · Map first, cards beneath

The map is the page. Coloured pins for the three kinds; the controls float over the top; the results are a carousel along the bottom that follows the map. List is a toggle.

Why this shape

  • "Is it near the theatre?" is answered before anything else, which is the question that decides a day out.

Trade-off

  • Two at a time; slow to scan sixty places. The list toggle is doing real work.
  • Most new building of the three.

The same page wide: filters down the left, cards in a grid, sources as one control in the top bar.
