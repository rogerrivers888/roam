Trips tab — three ways to cut it down

Next page: Shortlist → Settle → Trip (calling, booking, ordering, travel times, saving to the trip).

Each option is a coherent direction covering both screens you raised: the Trips home (what you see first) and the day view (where you build the journey). All three share the same rules: only trips in the next two weeks are in view, no country grouping on the home screen, one “Shortlist” action per place, places are never lost (set aside, not removed), and travel time between consecutive stops is shown for walk, car, taxi and tube. Every trip gets a Delete.

Option 1 · “Up next” list + journey box

The most conservative cut. The home is a short list with the next fortnight open and everything else folded. The day is three stacked boxes: Journey, Find places, Plan it for me. Closest to today’s code, so quickest to ship.

Find · three dropdowns and one action per row

What goes

  • Pace segmented control, time bar, “Getting around today”, price row, chains row.
  • Morning / afternoon / evening slots and the Unscheduled card.
  • “Add to the day” button. Shortlisting on a day puts it on the journey.
  • Overview and Map tabs; Where / What / When filters on the home.

What stays

  • Best match / Top rated / Most reviewed / Nearest, now a dropdown.
  • Cuisine and kind chips, chains, radius and sources, behind Filters.
  • The detail drawer.

Trade-off

  • Map is small on a phone; you scroll past it to reorder.
  • Least new code. Ships first.

Option 2 · Week strip + Journey / Find tabs

The home is anchored on a calendar: a fortnight strip with dots on the days that have something, trips for that fortnight beneath, and everything else behind one “All trips” row that opens the old filtered list. The day keeps a small map pinned at the top, and two tabs underneath: Journey and Find, so you can flick between them without losing the map.

Day · Find tab, results also on the map

What’s different from Option 1

  • Home shows time first: clashes like two Saturday outings are visible, which today’s list hides.
  • Map never scrolls away. Results appear as purple pins while you search, so “is it on the way?” is answered before you shortlist.
  • “Plan for me” is a third tab rather than a link, so it is discoverable without being in the way.

Trade-off

  • Three tabs on the day plus five in the tab bar: more navigation than Option 1.
  • The fortnight strip needs the “All trips” list to survive underneath for Lisbon and past trips.

Option 3 · Straight into the next trip, map-led

No home list at all. The Trips tab opens on whatever is next. A switcher at the top lists the rest, grouped Up next / Later / Past. The day is a full-height map with a bottom sheet for the journey. Search floats over the map and results come back as a card carousel you swipe through, each one a pin.

Switcher · the only “list” in this option

What’s different

  • Zero taps to the thing you actually want on a Saturday morning: the map of today.
  • Detour is the first number on a result, drawn as a dashed spur from the route, which is what the research says makes “is it on the way?” verifiable.
  • The list of trips becomes a switcher, so USA / Italy / UK never appear together.

Trade-off

  • Most new work: full-height map, bottom sheet, carousel, detour calculation per result.
  • A trip with no stops opens on an empty map. Needs a good empty state.
  • Least like the rest of the app today.

Recommendation. Build Option 1 first: it removes everything you named, keeps the parts you like, and every piece of it carries straight into Options 2 and 3 later (the journey list, the legs, the dropdown row, the set-aside state). Borrow one thing from Option 3 on day one: the detour number on each result, because the routing is already wired for it.
