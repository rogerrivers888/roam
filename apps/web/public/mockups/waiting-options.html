The wait

Every search has dead air in it — the catchment is worked out, three sources are asked, the pool is filtered, and until all that lands there is nothing on screen but a spinner. This replaces the spinner with the actual search, drawn: the country, then the city, then the ground you can reach, then the areas inside it being looked through. Real coastline, real borough boundaries, real area names. Nothing here is a GIF — it is one SVG map, a few kilobytes, that can be drawn for any town Roam searches.

The rule underneath all three: the animation never decides how long you wait. It is capped by the search — if the answer lands early it compresses and gets out of the way, and under about a third of a second it never appears at all.

Why this one

  • Nothing is hidden, so nothing has to come back — the end of the search is not a screen change, it is a strip getting shorter.
  • Results can stream: the first eight places are on screen while the map is still sweeping the far side of the catchment.
  • Small enough that it can be the default on every search, not just the long ones.

Costs

  • 132px of map cannot hold area names legibly, so the areas go in the line underneath instead.
  • Less of a moment. If the wait is genuinely seven seconds, this is not enough to hold anybody.

What makes it feel real

  • The coastline is Natural Earth and the boroughs are the real GLA boundaries, simplified to about 40 points each. A hand-drawn squiggle would read as decoration; a real coast reads as a map.
  • The zoom is one continuous camera move, eased, never a cut — that is what sells "we are going somewhere", and it is why it must be slow at the start.
  • The lens genuinely magnifies the map under it, so the sweep is not a badge sliding about on top of a picture.
  • Pins have no names. Until the pool is ranked we do not know what to show, and licensed names are not ours to scatter on a map.

Costs

  • Needs about three seconds to land. On a fast search you see the first second of it and then it leaves — which is fine, but the fancy part is wasted.
  • Full takeover: it must not be the default, or Roam will feel slower than it is.

How it stays honest

The search sets the clock, not the animation

Three thresholds. Under 300ms nothing appears — the results are simply there. Between 300ms and the end of the timeline the beats compress (playback speeds up to 2.5×, minimum 350ms of outro) rather than cutting mid-zoom. Past the timeline the sweep loops until the last source answers.

Set the control above to 0.2s and no map is drawn at all.

Every beat is a real event

POST /api/discover already does the work in order: catchment from deriveCatchment, then searchAllSources, then applyConstraints. Streaming those four moments back — SSE, or a chunked JSON prelude — turns the beats into events instead of timings. A source that is slow says so by name; the line stays spinning and the map does not pretend it finished.

The areas are open data, so we may keep them

Administrative boundaries and area names come from OpenStreetMap, which sits in the owned layer with the rest of sources/own.js — the same rule that lets us hold a summary or a postcode district. A simplified boundary is a couple of kilobytes and is cached per town, so the second search of the evening draws instantly.

No provider's names, photos or ratings appear on the map. The pins are counts, not content.

Anywhere, not just London

The camera takes three boxes: country, town, catchment. Country outlines are one 8KB file for the whole world at sketch resolution; the town box comes from the geocode Roam already ran; the catchment is the polygon the search is bounded by. Padstow and Palermo get the same treatment.

Reduced motion, and the fifth search of the evening

With prefers-reduced-motion the map draws once, static, with the areas named and the lines ticking — no zoom, no sweep. Tick the box above to see it.

The full sketch (C) should run on the first search of a session and after that fall back to A, so the flourish never becomes a toll.

What it costs to build

One <Sketch> component: about 300 lines of SVG and a request-animation-frame camera, no library, no images, no GIF. It reads width from useViewport() so it works in the phone frame and wide. Icons stay Lucide; the magnifier is drawn geometry, not a glyph.

The honest part — streaming the four events out of /api/discover — is the larger half of the job.
