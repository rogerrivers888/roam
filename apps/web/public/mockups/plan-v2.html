Plan screen, second pass

Built on Option B (the rows are the screen) with Option A’s Inspire me. This page answers the five notes on the first pass: what happens to specific wants like climbing or a pub lunch then Italian; several things to check, not one; correcting a row by voice or by tapping into it; one control instead of Done + Send; and Inspire me with a query box and a drawer of things to do and see. The first pass is here.

1 · Specific wants land in the rows as you say them

“I want to go climbing” is not “2 activities”: it is a named thing in Do. “A pub lunch and then Italian for dinner” becomes two lines under Eat, one per meal, each with what you asked of it. Counts only appear when that is what you said (“one or two other things” → “+ 1–2 more”). While you talk there is one control: the red Stop. The box is small on purpose — the rows are what you watch.

1c · Stopped. The rows are complete, the two doubts are a numbered queue, and the one button is Plan it — dimmed until the queue is done. Send only appears when you have typed.

How a want becomes a row

  • A named thing (climbing, the Roman Baths, a boat trip) goes under Do by name. It is searched for by name and pinned; the day is built around it.
  • A named meal (“pub lunch”, “Italian for dinner”, “coffee first”) gets its own line under Eat, with the kind attached to that meal, not to the day.
  • A count (“two things”, “one or two more”) shows as “+ N more” under the named things, never instead of them.
  • A quality (“somewhere good”, “cheap and cheerful”, “no chains”) lands in Budget or as a note on the meal it was said about.
  • Anything the words leave open (indoor/outdoor, how long) is a ? on the row and a question in the queue — not a guess.

2 · Several things to check, in a queue

Doubts come as a numbered list, one open at a time. Answer by tapping or by saying it. The answered one collapses to a line with its answer; the next opens; when the queue is empty the card turns into “All set” and Plan it lights up. You can skip one — the plan proceeds with the row left open.

Rules

  • One question open at a time, in the order they matter (place before time before who).
  • Every answer is a chip and a sentence: tap it or say it.
  • Answers are written straight into the row they were about, so the rows are always the truth.
  • Skip is allowed; the row keeps its ? and the plan carries on without it.

3 · Correcting a row: say it, or tap into it

Two ways, same result. Say it: “No, not the Peak District, the Lake District” — the planner matches the correction to the row it is about and rewrites it; you never have to name the row. Tap it: the row opens in place with the choices for that field, a Say it again mic for just that row, and Done. Nothing else on the screen moves.

3c · Tap into Eat. One line per meal with its kind, and the price point once for the day. The chips are the household’s own likes first.

How a correction finds its row

  • “Not X, Y” or “you got that wrong, it’s Y” — the planner finds the row whose value matches X and replaces it. If two rows could match, it asks which (a check, not a guess).
  • “Actually, make it Sunday” — a value on its own is matched by kind: a date → When, a cuisine → Eat, a place → To.
  • Tapping a row and speaking scopes the words to that row only, so “Italian” cannot land anywhere else.
  • Every change shows “was: …” for one turn, so a wrong correction is caught too.

4 · Inspire me: a query, ideas, and a drawer of things to do and see

Inspire me has its own box: type or say a loose brief, pick a mood or two, cap the travel. Ideas come back as cards that say why. Tap one and a drawer opens (a side drawer on the web, a sheet on the phone) with what there is to do and see there — each item can be added to the plan’s rows, or the whole day planned in one go. Tap into any item for its own detail the same way.

Where the ideas come from

  • Your atlas first: places you saved, loved or put on the list, and cities you have been to — each idea says which of those it rests on.
  • Then the sources, inside the travel cap, filtered by mood and by the household’s tastes and allergens as usual.
  • One small model call turns pills + query + atlas into ideas; the drawer’s list is the ordinary place search for that spot (no extra model call).
  • Nothing here is booked or saved unless you say so: Plan this day only fills the rows.
