Inspire me — a cleaner form, three ways

The form today is nine mood pills, four travel pills, five budget pills, a Who line, a box and two buttons: twenty-two things to look at before you ask. Three ways to cut it down. In all three, who is coming moves up into the header as an assumption ("The family · from home"), the moods that cannot go together (Easygoing / Intensive / Relaxing) become one pick, budget is a slider, and travel gets its own line so "from home" is not buried among pills. The rows and pills that stay are the ones from the third-pass Plan screen, so Tell me and Inspire me read as one screen.

A · Three rows, like Tell me

Inspire me is the same shape as Tell me: a header with the assumption line, a short box, and rows you tap. Mood is one row (a single pick, with an optional "and…"), Travel and Budget share a row as two halves, each opening a slider. One button.

Travel & Budget open. The two sliders sit together: distance on one, a from–to on the other with the spread of what days like this cost, the same control as the Plan screen's Budget row.

What changes

  • Who's coming moves into the header as "The family · from home" with the faces; tap to untick someone or start elsewhere. It is the same line Tell me has.
  • Mood is one row. Tapping it opens a list: Easygoing / Fun / Relaxing / Full-on as the pace (pick one), then Outdoors / Culture / Food / Somewhere new as the kind (pick one, optional). The row reads "Fun and Outdoors".
  • Travel and Budget share a row, split in two, and open a panel with two sliders: distance from home, and a from–to for the day (For everyone / Per person). "From home" is in the slider's own label, not floating after the pills.
  • The box is shorter and optional. Speak and one button.

Trade-off

Two taps to set a mood instead of one, but the screen is calm and it matches Tell me row for row.

Nothing on screen but one question at a time, three or four big tiles each, and a strip of what you have picked so far. Three steps: how the day should feel, what kind, then how far and how much (the two sliders). Ideas come straight after the third.

What changes

  • One question per screen, three or four tiles each, so nothing competes. On the phone each step fits without scrolling.
  • The strip carries the picks forward; tapping one goes back to that step.
  • Step 3 is the two sliders and an optional line of words, then the button. Speak is on every step and skips the rest.
  • Coming back to Inspire me later starts at step 3 with the last picks in the strip, so a second go is one tap.

Trade-off

Three taps before ideas. The least cluttered, and the most guided; on the web the three steps can sit side by side in one row instead.

The first blank open: one pick from four, each with a line on what it means. The second blank (outdoors / culture / food / somewhere new) works the same; the budget blank opens the slider panel from A.

What changes

  • Four dropdowns in a sentence replace eighteen pills. Each is a single pick, so the ones that clash cannot both be on.
  • "From home" is part of the sentence, next to the distance where it belongs; tapping it is the same as tapping the header line.
  • Budget opens the from–to slider; the blank shows the range.
  • What you said aloud lands in the same blanks: "somewhere fun, cheap, within an hour" fills three of them.

Trade-off

Dropdowns hide the choices until tapped, so a first-time user sees less of what is possible. Reads best in English; the wording has to be written for each blank.

On the web

The same controls at 680 wide. In A and C the row and the sentence simply have more room; in B the three steps sit in one line as three columns, no stepping.
